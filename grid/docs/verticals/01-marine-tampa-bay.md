# Vertical 1 — Marine mechanics, Tampa Bay (build this one)

Working brand: **Tampa Bay Boat Mechanics** (check domain availability; alternatives: Bay Mobile
Marine, Gulf Coast Boat Techs). Local 727 number. Site in `sites/boat/`.

## The market

**Where the boats are.** Pinellas, Hillsborough, and Pasco together hold roughly 110,000 to
120,000 registered vessels (approximate, from FLHSMV's annual vessel statistics by county; confirm
the current year's PDF on the FLHSMV Vessel Owner Statistics page). Florida as a whole is near one
million. Pinellas is the densest boating county in the region: a peninsula with the Gulf on one side
and the bay on the other, thousands of canal-front homes with lifts, and large dry-stack
operations.

**Where they launch and sit.** Tarpon Springs (Anclote River, Anclote River Park and Fred Howard
Park ramps, the Sponge Docks), Palm Harbor and Ozona, Dunedin (Dunedin Causeway, Honeymoon Island,
city marina), Clearwater (Seminole Boat Ramp with eight lanes, Clearwater Harbor marinas),
Safety Harbor (Philippe Park), Largo and Seminole (Intracoastal, War Veterans' Memorial Park ramp),
the beach towns from Indian Rocks to Pass-a-Grille (John's Pass), St. Petersburg (Demens Landing,
Maximo Park, Municipal Marina, Harborage, Fort De Soto with 24 lanes), Gulfport, Tampa (Davis
Islands, Ballast Point, Courtney Campbell ramp, Hillsborough River), Apollo Beach and Ruskin
(Little Manatee River, E.G. Simmons Park, Williams Park, canal homes with lifts), New Port Richey
and Port Richey (Pithlachascotee River, Nick's Park), Hudson (Hudson Beach, SunWest).

**Season.** Year-round, which is the point. Peaks March to September. Bay scallop season on the
Pasco and Hernando flats (July to September) fills the ramps north of Anclote. Snowbirds November
to April bring older boats that sat all summer. Hurricane season June to November creates two
spikes: storm prep in advance and inspections and repairs after. Helene and Milton (2024) left 343
derelict vessels in the Tampa Bay area and displaced marinas such as Gulfport's for over a year;
storm-damaged boats were still working through shops in 2025 and 2026.

## The customer and the jobs

| Customer | Where | Typical jobs |
|---|---|---|
| Lift-kept center console behind a canal home | Apollo Beach, Tarpon, Dunedin, St. Pete canals | 100/300-hour service, won't start after sitting, batteries, corrosion, trim/tilt |
| Slip-kept cruiser or sailboat | St. Pete marinas, Clearwater, Tampa | Inboard/diesel service, electrical, fuel polishing, A/C and systems |
| Trailer boat at a ramp | Ruskin, Hudson, Pasco | Outboard repair, trailer bearings and brakes, fuel system from ethanol |
| Dry-stack boat | Clearwater, St. Pete, Tampa | Routine service coordinated with the yard, electronics installs |

**Common failures by brand** (feeds the brand pages and the quote defaults): Yamaha F-series VST
filters and fuel pumps, thermostats, low-pressure fuel pumps on older F150s; Mercury Verado
supercharger service and oil, OptiMax air compressors; Suzuki lean-burn sensitivity to ethanol
fuel and water pumps; Honda BF water pumps and thermostats; legacy Evinrude E-TEC parts scarcity;
MerCruiser and Volvo sterndrive bellows, manifolds and risers in salt water; Yanmar raw-water
pumps and heat exchangers on sail and trawler diesels. Everywhere: ethanol-related fuel problems,
water pump impellers, batteries and corroded grounds.

## Prices in this market

Published mobile marine labor runs about $120 to $150 an hour in Pinellas and Hillsborough. A
300-hour outboard service lands $900 to $2,000 depending on engine, often around $1,500 at shops.
Mobile techs charge a trip or diagnostic fee of roughly $75 to $150. Average ticket through the
platform should settle around $600 to $700, which makes the 10% fee about $65 a job before
processing. Techs set their own numbers; these are sanity ranges.

## Competition

- **Local mobile mechanics**, most solo with few reviews: HouseCall Marine (Tarpon Springs), Your
  Mobile Marine (Tarpon Springs), Mr. Boatworks (Pinellas/Pasco/Hillsborough), Waterside Marine
  (Clearwater, "24/7"), A Plus Mobile Marine (St. Pete), Poseidon Mobile Marine, Mobile Marina,
  Executive Mechanic, the Largo Suzuki dealer's mobile unit. These are recruits as much as rivals.
- **Dealers and yards** with multi-week waits in season.
- **Marketplaces:** SeaRepair (national, free posting, techs contact owners), Boatwork (national
  directory plus storefront subscriptions), BoatGrid (SW Florida, zero public techs). None has a
  Tampa Bay presence or a dispatch desk that answers in five minutes.

**Positioning:** the dispatch desk. "Post it or call it. We answer in five minutes, send an insured
mechanic who knows your engine, and you pay by bank or card when it's done." Not a directory, not
a shop.

## Legal and compliance specific to marine

- No Florida state license for marine mechanics. Boat lift, dock, seawall and piling work requires
  a marine contractor license in Pinellas and other counties: **excluded from the service list.**
- Techs need a county business tax receipt (Hillsborough, Pasco) or city receipt (Clearwater and
  most Pinellas cities); unincorporated Pinellas has none.
- Marinas commonly require a certificate of insurance naming them before a mobile tech works on
  site. Techs handle that; HQ keeps the certificate and expiry.
- Sales tax: repair with parts is fully taxable in Florida; labor-only is not. The quote and invoice
  carry the county rate (Pinellas 7%, Hillsborough 7.5%, Pasco 7%; verify current surtax).
- Environmental: oil and fuel handling at the dock is the tech's responsibility; the agreement says so.

## Risks specific to this vertical

- Parts lead times stretch jobs; techs quote labor firm and parts at cost plus markup.
- Techs poaching: the 12-month clause plus steady new leads is the only real defense.
- Storms: a named storm pauses everything for a week, then doubles demand. Plan cash accordingly.
- Reviews: one bad tech can sink the Google profile early; pause after the first serious complaint,
  not the third.
