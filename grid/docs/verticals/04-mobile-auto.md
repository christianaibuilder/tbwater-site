# Vertical 4 — Mobile auto mechanics, Tampa Bay (plan; unlock at level 5)

Working brand: **Bay Area Mobile Mechanics**. Starter services and brands in
`VERTICAL_TEMPLATES.auto`. Launched last on purpose: biggest market, most competition, most rules.

## The market

Three million people, two cars per household, and the most-searched repair terms of any vertical.
Also the most crowded: YourMechanic (now part of Wrench) lists hundreds of Tampa reviews, Thumbtack
has dozens of mobile mechanics with published $50 to $120 service calls, and every neighborhood has
a guy with a truck. Winning here is not about being first; it is about a narrower promise.

**Where the model has an edge:**

| Segment | Why it fits |
|---|---|
| Small-business fleets (landscapers, plumbers, pool and water-treatment vans, delivery) | Scheduled on-site maintenance, one invoice, one payer; TB Water's own vans are customer zero |
| No-start and brakes at home | Highest-intent searches; mobile saves a tow |
| Pre-purchase inspections | Low liability, fixed price, repeat referrals from used-car buyers |
| Check-engine diagnostics with a quote | Diagnostic-first is what customers already expect |

## Jobs and tickets

| Job | Typical ticket |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic visit | $90 to $150 (often credited to the repair) |
| No-start: battery, starter, alternator | $250 to $650 |
| Brakes (pads and rotors, one axle) | $300 to $600 |
| Oil change and maintenance | $90 to $200 |
| Cooling system | $300 to $800 |
| Suspension and steering | $400 to $900 |
| Pre-purchase inspection | $120 to $200 |
| Fleet visit (3 to 5 vehicles) | $500 to $1,200 |

Average ticket is the lowest of the four, around $300 to $450. The $25 minimum fee is essential,
and a 12% rate or $30 minimum is defensible here because the lead cost to a solo mechanic is high.

## Laws that matter (this vertical has real ones)

- **Florida Motor Vehicle Repair Act** (Fla. Stat. 559.901 to 559.9221, enforced by FDACS). Anyone
  repairing vehicles for compensation must **register with FDACS every two years**; mobile mechanics
  and self-employed individuals are explicitly covered. Fee is tiered by employees: $50 for 1 to 5.
  Collect the registration number at application and show it on the tech's profile.
- **Written estimates** are required when the final bill will exceed **$150** (the current FDACS
  form; older material still says $100). The estimate must include specific items (customer's
  name, vehicle, odometer, description of the problem, proposed work, estimated cost, whether used
  or rebuilt parts, the customer's choice on keeping old parts). The platform's quote flow is built
  to satisfy this; add the required fields to the auto category's quote template before launch.
- Work exceeding the estimate by more than $10 or 10% (sources differ on the cap) requires the
  customer's approval first; the quote-accept and change-order flow covers it.
- Invoices must itemize parts and labor and state warranty terms. The invoice extraction keeps
  line items; add a warranty field for this category.
- Sales tax on repairs with parts, as elsewhere.
- Emissions and safety: Florida has no inspections, which simplifies things.

## Competition and pricing

YourMechanic/Wrench with published upfront pricing, ASE-certified techs, strong Tampa review
counts; Thumbtack listings at $50 to $120 per call; independents like Lonnie's Mobile Mechanic
(calls from $50, after-hours $75) and Auto Repair Mobile Mechanic (diagnostic $75 to $120 credited
to repair). Complaints in reviews cluster around surprise diagnostic charges; the written-estimate
rule, enforced by the platform, is a selling point.

## Supply plan

Target 10 techs with FDACS registration, ASE certifications preferred, their own scan tools, and
insurance that covers mobile automotive work (garagekeepers coverage for vehicles in their care).
Sources: Thumbtack and Facebook Marketplace mechanics, shop techs moonlighting, fleet mechanics,
trade school graduates (Pinellas Technical College, Erwin Technical College).

## Demand plan

- **Fleets first.** Ten small businesses with 3 to 10 vehicles each, sold by phone and by TB Water's
  own trade network, give a base load that doesn't depend on Google. Monthly on-site maintenance
  day per fleet.
- Then no-start and brakes via Google (search ads on "mobile mechanic near me", "car won't start
  Tampa", "mobile brake repair Clearwater") and the Google Business Profile.
- Pre-purchase inspections via used-car dealer lots and Facebook Marketplace car listings.
- Keep ad spend tight; cost per click in auto repair runs several dollars and competition is deep.

## Targets

First 90 days: 10 fleet accounts, 10 techs, 40 paid jobs a month by month three, average ticket
$400. If fleet traction is weak by day 60, keep the vertical fleet-only and don't chase consumer
search.

## Risks

Highest liability of the four (vehicles, roads, brakes); require garagekeepers and higher general
liability limits. Regulatory exposure if estimate rules are skipped; the platform must enforce,
not suggest. Parts logistics and warranty disputes. Price competition from national players with
venture budgets.
