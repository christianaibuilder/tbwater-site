# 07 — Outreach playbook: techs first, then customers, then speed

Two motions. Supply is easy and should be done one to two weeks before demand is switched on.
Demand is the job.

---

## Part 1 — Recruiting technicians (supply)

### Target roster for the boat vertical

| Need | Why |
|---|---|
| 10 to 12 approved techs | Enough that every offer has three matches without burning anyone out |
| Coverage: 4 Pinellas, 4 Hillsborough, 2 to 3 Pasco | Matches where the boats are |
| Brands: at least 3 Yamaha, 3 Mercury, 2 Suzuki, 1 Honda/Tohatsu, 2 inboard/diesel, 1 electrical specialist | Matches what breaks |
| At least 6 with a truck and tools for dockside work | Mobile is the product |

### Where to find them (in order of yield)

1. **Google Maps.** Search "mobile marine mechanic", "boat mechanic", "outboard repair" for
   Tarpon Springs, Palm Harbor, Clearwater, Largo, St. Petersburg, Tampa, Apollo Beach, New Port
   Richey, Hudson. Every solo operator with under 30 reviews is a prospect. Several found in
   research: HouseCall Marine, Your Mobile Marine, Mr. Boatworks, Waterside Marine, A Plus Mobile
   Marine, and the Suzuki dealer in Largo's mobile unit.
2. **Facebook Marketplace and groups.** Search "mobile marine mechanic" in the services section
   filtered to 30 miles. Post in boating groups: "Looking for two more insured mobile mechanics for
   overflow work in North Pinellas."
3. **Thumbtack and Angi.** Pros paying $35 to $85 per lead are the easiest sell on earth.
4. **Dealer service departments.** Former dealer techs who went mobile are the best techs. Ask
   current service writers who left recently.
5. **Marinas and dry storage.** The yard manager knows every independent who works the lot.
6. **Marine trade school boards** (Manatee Technical College marine service program, Pinellas
   Technical College) for younger techs building a book.

### The pitch (phone, 90 seconds)

> "I run a small dispatch service for boat owners in Tampa Bay. Owners post a job, I vet it, and I
> send it to two or three insured mechanics near them. Free to join, no monthly fee. You set your own
> rates and you can say no to any job. When you finish, you snap a photo of your invoice; we collect
> from the customer by bank or card and pay you 90% once it clears. We handle the quote chasing and
> the billing. If I send you two jobs a month you'd never have seen, is that worth ten percent?"

### Objections you will hear

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "10% is a lot" | Thumbtack charges $35 to $85 per lead whether or not you win it. We charge only on paid work, and we chase the money. |
| "I don't want to wait to get paid" | Bank payments clear in about four business days; cards in two. We pay the same day it clears. Ask the tech to compare with how long their own invoices take. |
| "I have enough work" | Perfect. Send us the jobs you turn away and we'll send you the ones that fit. (That becomes the overflow program below.) |
| "What if the customer doesn't pay?" | The customer pays through the link before you're owed anything; you're never carrying the receivable. |
| "Do I lose the customer?" | Repeat work from a customer we introduced runs through the platform for 12 months. After that, they're yours. |

### Onboarding checklist (HQ enforces the money parts)

1. Phone call done; confirm they can decline jobs and set prices (classification hygiene).
2. Insurance certificate emailed; expiry entered.
3. W-9 received. No payout without it.
4. Agreement accepted (checkbox at application, version recorded).
5. Zips, specialties, brands entered accurately. Matching depends on it.
6. Portal invite sent; they accept one test offer so they know the flow.
7. Business tax receipt number noted (their responsibility).

### Keep-warm rule

A tech who joins and hears nothing for three weeks is gone. Recruit the first six in the week before
ads start, add the rest as volume grows. Text every tech once a week in month one even if it's
"quiet week, two jobs coming in Clearwater".

### Overflow program

Every tech turns away work in season. Ask each one to text you those customers' numbers (with the
customer's OK). You dispatch to someone else and the referring tech is remembered when the next job
in their area comes up. That is free demand, from day one.

## Part 2 — Getting customers (demand)

### Channel plan, first 90 days

| Channel | Cost | Lead speed | First action |
|---|---|---|---|
| TB Water customer list | $0 | Days | One email and one postcard: "We started a vetted boat-mechanic service." |
| Google Search Ads | $25 to $40/day | Days | Exact-match campaign, see below |
| Google Business Profile | $0 | 2 to 6 weeks to verify | Start video verification on day one |
| Facebook groups + Marketplace | $0 to $10/day | Days | Join 10 groups, service listing, answer "anyone know a mechanic" posts |
| Physical: ramps, dry storage, bait shops | $80 in flyers | Weeks | QR flyers with a tracking number |
| Marinas without service departments | $0 | Weeks | Visit with a one-page sheet for the office |
| Tech overflow | $0 | Immediate | Ask every approved tech |
| SEO pages | $0 | 3 to 6 months | Submit sitemap, then one page a week |

### Google Ads, exactly

- Campaign type: Search. Location: Pinellas, Hillsborough, Pasco. Schedule: 7am to 8pm.
- Ad groups by intent, exact and phrase match only:
  - `mobile boat mechanic`, `boat mechanic near me`, `outboard mechanic near me`
  - `yamaha outboard repair`, `mercury outboard repair`, `suzuki outboard repair`
  - `boat won't start`, `outboard won't start`, `boat overheating`
  - `boat electrical repair`, `marine electrician`
  - `boat mechanic clearwater` (and each town)
- Negatives: `jobs`, `salary`, `school`, `course`, `parts`, `used`, `for sale`, `rental`, `jet ski`.
- Landing page: the matching service or brand page, never the home page. Form above the fold.
- Call extension with the tracking number. Most boat owners call.
- Budget math: at $4 to $8 a click and a 10 to 15% form-or-call rate, a lead costs $30 to $80.
  At a 35% close rate and a $650 average ticket, a paid job costs about $100 to $230 in ads and
  earns $60 to $65 in platform fee. **Ads lose money per job at first.** They buy reviews, techs'
  trust, and the data that tells you which pages to write. Cap at $1,000 a month and cut it as the
  map pack and pages take over.

### Facebook, exactly

- Groups to join: Tampa Bay boating and fishing groups, Pinellas and Pasco neighborhood groups,
  Apollo Beach and Ruskin waterfront community groups, Dunedin and Tarpon Springs locals groups.
  Read rules; most allow business recommendations in reply to a request, not cold posts.
- Turn on notifications for keywords "mechanic", "outboard", "boat won't". Reply within minutes
  with a useful answer first, then "we can get someone out tomorrow, here's the number."
- Marketplace: one service listing per county, renewed weekly, with a real job photo.
- Page ad: $10 a day, 30-mile radius, interests boating and fishing, objective calls. Creative is a
  photo of a tech at a dock and the line "Boat mechanic to your dock. Answered in 5 minutes."

### Physical, exactly

- Flyers with a QR code to the post-a-job page and the tracking number at: Anclote River Park and
  Fred Howard Park ramps (Tarpon Springs), Dunedin Causeway and Marina, Seminole Boat Ramp
  (Clearwater), War Veterans' Memorial Park (Bay Pines), Demens Landing and Maximo Park (St. Pete),
  Davis Islands and Courtney Campbell (Tampa), Williams Park and E.G. Simmons Park (Ruskin), Nick's
  Park (Port Richey), Hudson Beach. Replace monthly; they get weathered.
- Dry storage barns and marinas with no service department: a one-page sheet for the office, offer
  a 5% referral credit on the customer's next service.
- Cross-referral with detailers, canvas shops, prop shops, and boat lift companies: they see the
  problems first.
- Bait and tackle shops: counter card.

### TB Water list, exactly

One email, subject "Know a boat owner who can't find a mechanic?" Body: three sentences, the phone
number, the link. One postcard to the waterfront zips (34689, 34698, 34683, 33767, 33572, 33570,
33704, 33706). Twenty years of trust is the asset; use it once, politely.

### Events

- St. Petersburg Power & Sailboat Show, January 14 to 17, 2027: not as an exhibitor, as a visitor
  with cards, talking to small dealers who need a service partner.
- Tampa Bay Boat Show and Armature Works Boat Show: same approach.
- Hurricane season (June to November): a "storm prep" service push in May and a "post-storm
  inspection" push the week after any named storm. Post-Helene and Milton, Tampa Bay had hundreds
  of damaged and displaced boats; this is a recurring demand spike.

## Part 3 — Speed to lead (the operating standard)

- Phone on, always. New lead texts you and emails you. HQ shows the timer in red after 5 minutes.
- **First call script (2 minutes):** confirm boat, engine, hours, where it sits (lift, slip, trailer,
  which marina), what it's doing, when it started, photos by text. Tell them: "I'll have a mechanic
  reach out within the hour; you'll get his name by text." Log the call. Approve and offer.
- **Dispatch text to techs** goes out automatically. If no acceptance in 30 minutes, call the top
  match directly.
- **Quote follow-up:** day 1 text, day 3 call, day 7 text, then mark it.
- **After payout:** review request text with the Google link. Ask within 24 hours of payment; the
  rate drops by half after that.
- **Dead leads:** always record why. After 30 days, the pattern tells you what to fix (price,
  coverage gaps, slow techs).

### Daily rhythm (30 minutes, plus calls as they come)

Morning: clear new leads, chase open offers, send any quotes built overnight. Midday: review
invoices, send payment links. Evening: mark payouts paid, text the techs who need a nudge, look at
tomorrow's schedule.
